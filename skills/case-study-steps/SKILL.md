## Steps
1. Read what was given. Pull out the order of events: what was learned, what was built, how it was checked.
2. Group it into at most three steps. Default shape is learn, build, test. Merge or drop detail to fit. Three is the cap, two is fine.
3. Write each step as a label plus one sentence (format below).
4. Suggest a simple line icon per step and a pastel circle color (peach `#FFD9C2`, lavender `#CDBDEB`, sky `#BFD8EC`).
5. If the work is unfinished, add one "Up next" line. If the product is unpublished or unvalidated, add a one-line disclaimer under it.
6. Show it as a table and flag anything marked [CONFIRM]. Do not publish or edit a page until the owner says so.

## Example format (Aisle case study, approved by the owner)
- **Cracked the florist math**: Scanned the market, learned how florists really price, and interviewed other brides to see if they felt the same sticker shock.
- **Built the formula**: Turned that research into a pricing model using public wholesale prices, adjusted for city costs and florist markup.
- **Put it to the test**: Ran Pinterest bouquets through it, then had friends poke holes, from pricing errors to images that looked too AI.
- Up next: Checking the prices with real florists, and surveying brides to see if they would actually use the website.
- Disclaimer: Aisle is an unpublished prototype. Estimates are illustrative and not quotes. Aisle is a working name.

## Rules
- **No step numbers and no big stats column.** Label and sentence sit together beside an icon.
- **Label:** 2 to 4 words, past tense, a little playful, starts with a strong verb. Says what was done, not the topic.
- **Sentence:** one sentence, about 25 words at most, starts with a verb, no "I". Reads as if presented to an executive: no detail the owner would have to defend line by line.
- **Plain words.** No em dashes, no consultant-speak, no rule-of-three lists in the sentence.
- **Only claim what the files and the owner's words support.** Nothing validated, no accuracy, savings or user numbers unless the owner gave a source. Say "prototype", not "launched".
- **Leave out third parties** (named experts, competitors) unless the owner asks.
- **Mark anything uncertain** as [CONFIRM], such as counts of interviews.
- Check the owner's wording against what they actually did. If their edit introduces a typo or a leftover word, point it out and leave their text alone.

## One-line version (headlines and section intros)
- Same rules, compressed into one sentence of about 10 words. Plain, warm, first person, starts with what the owner does.
- Approved example, a home page services headline: "I start by listening, then work as part of your team."
- Give one pick plus two alternates, and say why the pick fits.
- **Service straplines:** say what the owner does, then what it becomes (input to outcome). Name a concrete object, not "answers" or "the work". About 10 to 14 words.
  - Approved: "Turn a complex project into a clear plan, then run it through delivery." / "Analyze the market and competitors, then recommend the move worth making." / "Turn dense analysis into the executive deck that gets a decision."
